Running the Plant

The 305-M test pile was the first reactor on the site to start up, in 1952, the year heavy-water production began in D Area (WB: srs.gov 1950s). R reactor started up in December 1953, P on February 20, 1954, and all five were running by the end of March 1955 (WB: SRS at Fifty ch. 11).

The separations plants took the irradiated fuel and targets from the reactors, dissolved them, and used chemistry to pull out the plutonium and other products, leaving the radioactive waste behind. F Area was the world's first full-scale plant to use this method, called Purex, and purified plutonium; H Area mostly recovered enriched uranium (WB: ch. 11).

F Area started in 1954 and H Area in 1955. Radioactive tritium went to the AEC (Atomic Energy Commission) from 1955, and the basic plant was complete in 1956 (WB: srs.gov 1950s; WB: History Highlights). The first shipment of radioactive plutonium is dated December 1954 by one official source and 1955 by others (WB: ch. 11; WB).

P reactor also served pure science. In 1955 and 1956 two Los Alamos physicists, Frederick Reines and Clyde Cowan, set up detectors beside it (WB: SRS at Fifty ch. 13). Reactors give off huge numbers of neutrinos, tiny particles with no electric charge that pass through almost everything (WB: Nobel Prize, 1995). In June 1956 they announced the first detection of the neutrino (WB: ch. 13). Reines shared the 1995 Nobel Prize in Physics for it; Cowan had died by then (WB: Nobel Prize).

Then demand for weapons materials fell. R reactor shut down in 1964 and L in 1968, both because less nuclear material was needed (DL: Senate hearing, 1983; WB: srs.gov 1960s). The heavy-water components test reactor also closed in December 1964, for a different reason: the AEC was turning away from heavy-water power reactors, and it was never restarted (WB: ch. 13).

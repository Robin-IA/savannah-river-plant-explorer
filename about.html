About this explorer

A history of the Savannah River Plant told from the record.

The Project

The Savannah River Plant was built by Du Pont for the Atomic Energy Commission from 1950 on land in Aiken and Barnwell counties, South Carolina, and made plutonium and tritium for nuclear weapons until its reactors were shut down in 1988. Renamed the Savannah River Site in 1989, it has since been a place of clean-up, waste treatment, research and, again, plutonium work.

The story is told in four chapters: building the plant, 1950–1969; the reactors under scrutiny, 1970–1992; the years after bomb making, 1992–2020; and what is happening at the site now. It was written by Democracy's Library staff to weave together government resources (Democracy's Library), website archives (the Wayback Machine) and Internet Archive collections.

Where the Sources Are Held

The story rests on 461 sources: 68 in Democracy's Library, 254 Wayback Machine captures, 121 other Internet Archive items, 15 TV News Archive clips and 3 Open Library records. See them all.

  • DL Democracy's Library. An archive.org item inside Democracy's Library. The ones cited here come mostly from US Government Documents; congressional hearings in U.S. Congressional Hearings and Hearings, Prints and Reports, many of them on microfiche; the Government Publishing Office; The Federal Register; Radiation Data and Reports; Forest Service and other federal library reports in FEDLINK; the National Institutes of Health Library; and US Supreme Court Records and Briefs.
  • WB Wayback Machine. A page or document saved by the Wayback Machine. Much of the site's own history, including the official history The Savannah River Site at Fifty and twenty years of news releases, is gone from the live web and survives only there. Wherever an outside page, report or record has a working Wayback capture, the explorer cites the capture rather than the live page, and names the original holder.
  • IA Internet Archive. An archive.org item outside Democracy's Library: books in the lending library, magazines and newspapers, and government documents in collections such as the DTIC archive and the CIA Reading Room.
  • TVA TV News Archive at the Internet Archive. A broadcast recorded by the Internet Archive’s TV News Archive, linked at the moment the plant comes up. The clips are gathered on On Television.
  • OL Open Library. A book with a record in Open Library, the Internet Archive’s catalog of books. The story says whether a copy can be read or borrowed there.
  • EXT Held elsewhere. Outside the Internet Archive, with no working Wayback capture; the holder is named, often the Department of Energy’s OSTI.

Dates Covered

From the Atomic Energy Commission's decision in 1950 to the present. The sources themselves run from 1950 newspapers to web pages captured in 2026.

Topics Covered

The choice of site and the towns moved off it, among them Ellenton and Dunbarton; building the plant; the reactors and separations plants; early ecology and the Savannah River Ecology Laboratory; life in the towns around the plant, from crowded schools to payments in place of taxes; what the plant did to the land and how it recovered; the L-Reactor restart and lawsuit; the 1988 disclosures and shutdowns; the change from Du Pont to Westinghouse; liquid waste and the glass-making plant; sealing the P and R reactors in place; the MOX plant and plutonium pits; the national laboratory; and how the site is remembered.

How It Was Built

Researched from archive.org search and full-text search, the Wayback Machine's CDX server and the OSTI catalog. The story and the source lists are written in Markdown, and a short Python script turns them into these pages. The timeline is drawn from the same story, event by event. The code and the source notes are on GitHub.

The downloadable PDF of the story is made from the same files, by printing the whole story to one document. Whenever the story changes, the PDF is remade in the same update, so the download always matches the website.

About this explorer

A history of the Savannah River Plant told from the record, with every source labelled by where it is held, so a reader can open it and check.

The project

The Savannah River Plant was built by Du Pont for the Atomic Energy Commission from 1950 on land in Aiken and Barnwell counties, South Carolina, and made plutonium and tritium for nuclear weapons until its reactors were shut down in 1988. Renamed the Savannah River Site in 1989, it has since been a place of clean-up, waste treatment, research and, again, plutonium work.

The story is told in three acts: building the plant, 1950–1969; the reactors under scrutiny, 1970–1992; and the years after bomb making, 1992 to now. It was written for Democracy's Library, whose aim is to be the most complete source for government documents, and it shows how much of one government site's history sits inside the Internet Archive, and where.

Where the sources are held

  • DL Democracy's Library. An archive.org item inside Democracy's Library: it matches the collection's scope, which takes in US Government Documents, Government Publications, NASA, government documents on microfilm and others.
  • IA Internet Archive. An archive.org item outside Democracy's Library: books in the lending library, magazines and newspapers, and government documents in collections such as the DTIC archive and the CIA Reading Room.
  • WB Wayback Machine. A page or document saved by the Wayback Machine. Much of the site's own history, including the official history The Savannah River Site at Fifty and twenty years of news releases, is gone from the live web and survives only there.
  • AIT Archive-It. Web collections built by libraries and archives with Archive-It.
  • EXT Held elsewhere. Outside the Internet Archive; the holder is named, most often the Department of Energy's OSTI.

Dates covered

From the Atomic Energy Commission's decision in 1950 to the present. The sources themselves run from 1950 newspapers to web pages captured in 2026.

Topics covered

The choice of site and the towns moved off it, among them Ellenton and Dunbarton; building the plant; the reactors and separations plants; early ecology and the Savannah River Ecology Laboratory; the L-Reactor restart and lawsuit; the 1988 disclosures and shutdowns; the change from Du Pont to Westinghouse; liquid waste and the glass-making plant; sealing the P and R reactors in place; the MOX plant and plutonium pits; the national laboratory; and how the site is remembered.

When the numbers were taken

Every source was checked live on 6 November 2026: each archive.org item was fetched, each Democracy's Library label was re-tested against the collection's scope, and each Wayback capture and outside link was opened. The archives keep growing, so a source labelled IA today may be in Democracy's Library later.

What is left out

  • The New York Times series of autumn 1988 on the weapons plants: no article was verified.
  • The Hagley Museum and Library's Du Pont records and the Library of Congress engineering survey (HAER) could not be checked directly.
  • Archive-It captures could not be listed; only the collections' seeds are confirmed.
  • Facts that could not be traced to a source are left out of the story, and the places where sources disagree are set out at its end rather than resolved.

How it was built

Researched from archive.org search and full-text search, the Wayback Machine's CDX server and the Archive-It and OSTI catalogues. The story and the source lists are written in Markdown, and a short Python script turns them into these pages. The code and the source notes are on GitHub.

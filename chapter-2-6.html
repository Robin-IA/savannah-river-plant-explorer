Living with the Plant

The plant was the area's anchor employer. A 1984 Energy Department report put its permanent workforce at 9,200 in December 1982, most of them working for Du Pont, with plant workers and their families making up about half of Aiken's 15,000 people. The same report found that Aiken and Richmond counties ranked in the top half nationally for income in 1974, while most counties around the plant ranked in the bottom 11 percent (IA: DOE, 1984).

The 1980s buildup brought the workforce to 18,635 by May 1989, and a 1990 DOE study said shutting the K, L and P reactors would cost about 9,600 jobs (IA: DOE, 1990). In 1992 South Carolina listed Westinghouse Savannah River, with 23,000 workers, as its largest manufacturing employer, and noted that federal cutbacks had begun (IA: South Carolina Economic Report, 1993).

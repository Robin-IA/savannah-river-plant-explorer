Under the ground

What the plant had poured into its seepage basins came back through the groundwater. Reviewing permit applications in 1981, South Carolina's health and environment department judged that the volumes sent to the basins strongly suggested groundwater problems; in 1983 the plant reported that a well supplying drinking water to its own employees was contaminated, and two more followed (DL).

The department's deputy commissioner told Senator Glenn's committee in 1987 that plant staff had at first been reluctant to accept the state's regulatory authority (DL).

GAO found solvents in the groundwater under the M Area fuel plant at more than 30,000 times proposed drinking-water standards. Tritium at the separations areas was more than 2,500 times the standard. DOE answered that the contamination stayed under federal land and posed no threat to the public (IA: GAO 1986).

By 1991 the Office of Technology Assessment reported an EPA finding that the pump-and-treat system meant to clean the M Area plume was not adequately designed to reach its goal (DL).

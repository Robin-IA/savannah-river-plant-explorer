Living with the Plant

The payments in place of taxes still matter to the neighbors. In 2021, when the federal budget request for 2022 cut $5.7 million from them, Representative Jim Clyburn asked for the money back, and a Barnwell County council member called the payment "sort of like the sacred cow" (WB: Aiken Standard, 2021).

South Carolina's 2022–23 budget spent $525 million it listed as coming from the Savannah River Site litigation, including $105 million for new schools for Williston and Blackville (WB: South Carolina budget, 2022).

A state highway still crosses the site; its history is told in Chapter 1, Living with the Plant. South Carolina Highway 125, known along the way as Atomic Road, runs for about 17 miles across it between Allendale and Jackson: the site's directions for its public deer hunts send drivers from Allendale north through the site on it (WB: SRS; WB: Historical Marker Database).

In 2021 the state transportation department widened and repaved about 8 miles of it inside the site (WB: DOE). It is a road to drive through, not to stop on. The site's impact statements limit the public to "through traffic only" on Highway 125, U.S. Highway 278 and SRS Road 1. DOE controls access to the highway inside the site and can stop traffic and clear everyone off it quickly after an accident nearby (IA: DOE, 1990).

Site workers still give to local charities. The contractors reported raising $1,488,038 for United Way in the 2023 campaign (WB: SRS release), and the United Way of Allendale, Bamberg and Barnwell counties called the workers' gifts "a significant portion" of its budget (WB: contractor release, 2026). Both are the contractors' own announcements.

The land carries the site's history too, the subject of Ecology and the SRS.

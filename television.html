On Television

The plant on television: news reports, debates and hearings documented in the TV News Archive at the Internet Archive.

Each clip opens at the moment the plant comes up and plays at the TV News Archive. The summaries were checked against the archive's own transcripts on November 8, 2026. Most clips are from 2009 on, when the archive's recording began; older events appear when they were rebroadcast. Clips are tagged TVA on the Sources page.

Chapter 2. The Reactors Under Scrutiny, 1970–1992

  • TVA C-SPAN3 · Aired October 9, 2016; recorded October 13, 1988

    1988 Presidential Candidates Second Debate

    A rebroadcast of the second Bush–Dukakis debate. Michael Dukakis asks why the plants that make tritium and plutonium are being closed for safety violations; George H. W. Bush answers that the Savannah River plant needs to be made safer and suggests another plant, perhaps in Idaho.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: 1987–1988: The Reactors Stop

Chapter 3. After the Bombs, 1992–2020

  • TVA CNBC · December 4, 2009

    The Call

    A live report from the site on Recovery Act money spent cleaning up and sealing Cold War facilities, with 1,400 jobs created and 800 saved.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Sealing the Reactors

  • TVA MSNBC · December 4, 2009

    MSNBC News Live

    The same reporter, outside a shut-down reactor: $1.6 billion in stimulus money to clean the site, seal it and let it sit.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Sealing the Reactors

  • TVA CNN · February 3, 2010

    CNN Newsroom

    The Savannah River Site's $1.4 billion cleanup award was the largest single contract of the stimulus; the segment compares it with Hanford and counts the jobs each created.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Sealing the Reactors

  • TVA MSNBC · April 1, 2011

    The Rachel Maddow Show

    During Japan's 2011 nuclear crisis, the site sends a 70-meter concrete pump, refitted to pump water, to Japan.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: The Laboratories

  • TVA MSNBC · January 19, 2012

    The Daily Rundown

    Before South Carolina's 2012 primary, a guest names the waste left at the Savannah River Site as a cost of not opening Yucca Mountain.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: The Tanks

  • TVA Fox News · September 6, 2012

    America Live

    A South Carolina guest names the Savannah River plant among the federal employers that make the government the state's largest employer.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Living with the Plant

  • TVA Fox News · August 18, 2015

    America's Newsroom

    A brief item: the site was locked down after a bomb-sniffing dog found suspected residue on a delivery truck.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Living with the Plant

  • TVA WIS (NBC, Columbia) · February 12, 2016

    Local news during NBC Nightly News

    Before South Carolina's 2016 Republican primary, the candidates are asked about the administration's latest action on the MOX plant.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Plutonium: MOX and Pits

  • TVA WSOC (ABC, Charlotte) · March 21, 2016

    Eyewitness News Daybreak

    The federal government sues Savannah River Nuclear Solutions, the site's manager, saying it overcharged by more than $5 million since 2008. Earlier in the same program (16:00), British ships begin carrying plutonium from Japan to the site.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Plutonium: MOX and Pits

  • TVA Al Jazeera America · March 31, 2016

    News

    A field report from Atomic Road: Governor Nikki Haley tries to stop a shipment of plutonium from Japan; SRS Watch, a site engineer and a woman living downriver give their views, from terrorism risk to cancer in her family.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Plutonium: MOX and Pits

  • TVA WJLA (ABC, Washington) · June 25, 2017

    Full Measure With Sharyl Attkisson

    A report on the cost of cleaning up the weapons plants: a federal watchdog's 2017 high-risk report puts DOE's cleanup tab at $372 billion, about half of it for Hanford and the Savannah River Site.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: The Tanks

  • TVA C-SPAN3 · December 15, 2020

    Armed Services Committee Chair Rep. Adam Smith Discusses Nuclear Modernization

    Answering a question from Tom Clements of SRS Watch, the House Armed Services chair calls the MOX project "pretty close to white collar crime", says about $5 billion to $6 billion was spent on it, and doubts the building can become a pit plant.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Plutonium: MOX and Pits

Chapter 4. What's Happening Now, 2020 to Today

  • TVA C-SPAN · January 14, 2021

    South Carolina Governor Delivers State of the State Address

    South Carolina's governor proposes setting aside the $525 million South Carolina received from its Savannah River Site settlement with the federal government.

    Watch at the TV News Archive · Related in the story: Living with the Plant

  • TVA C-SPAN · October 26, 2023

    U.S. House of Representatives

    The House debates Representative John Garamendi's amendment to pause $850 million for the pit plant, which he calls a money pit; Representatives Fleischmann, Kaptur, Duncan and Allen speak against it.

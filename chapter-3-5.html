Sick Workers

Compensation for the plant's sick workers came slowly. At a 2003 House hearing Representative Charlie Norwood of Georgia said more than 300 of his constituents had filed claims over work at the site, and only a handful had a decision. The building trades' statement said Savannah River workers were treated unfairly under the program (DL: House hearing, 2003).

The 2012 class that spared early workers individual dose reconstruction was extended in 2021 to subcontracted construction workers through 1990 (WB: Federal Register) and in 2026 through 1993 (EXT: Federal Register).

Studies of the workers kept finding leukemia. A 1995 Oak Ridge study found that the site's workers died at lower rates than the general public but had significantly more leukemia (WB: site epidemiology summary). In 2007 NIOSH (National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health)-funded researchers following 18,883 workers hired from 1950 to 1986 found leukemia risk rising with recorded radiation dose, and excess cancer of the lung lining, often linked to asbestos (WB: NIOSH).

The records behind the claims had gaps. In 2007 NIOSH found that the site had not sent neutron radiation data for some claims (WB: NIOSH). That November a petition asked to add every worker from 1950 to 2007 to the Special Exposure Cohort; the special classes for the site came from it (WB: NIOSH).

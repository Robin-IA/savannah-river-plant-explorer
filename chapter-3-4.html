Plutonium: MOX and Pits

MOX (mixed oxide fuel) was a plan to turn surplus weapons plutonium into fuel for commercial nuclear power reactors, so it could no longer be used in bombs (DL: House hearing, 2006). South Carolina's fight over radioactive plutonium began before MOX. In April 2002 DOE (Department of Energy) decided to store surplus plutonium from Rocky Flats at Savannah River. Governor Jim Hodges sued, then issued an executive order banning plutonium from the state's roads, and in June a federal court barred him from interfering with the shipments; the Fourth Circuit found that DOE had not broken environmental law (DL: Hodges v. Abraham). He had vowed to lie in the road to stop them (IA).

Congress spent a decade on turning surplus plutonium into reactor fuel at Savannah River (DL; DL). DOE's 2016 baseline put the mixed-oxide plant at $17.17 billion, finished in 2048 — a document since removed from the watchdog site that posted it (WB: SRS Watch).

South Carolina sued to hold the government to its promises (WB: complaint), and The Post and Courier's source documents on the fight are on archive.org (IA). In May 2018 DOE certified that diluting and disposing of the plutonium would cost less than half of finishing MOX; the Fourth Circuit lifted the injunction keeping the project alive on January 8, 2019 (WB: 4th Cir.).

MOX workers had already been offered a job fair in December 2018 (WB). By August 2019 DOE reported a metric ton of plutonium removed from the state (WB: SC Attorney General). Half of that ton had gone, secretly, to Nevada.

DOE announced in August 2018 that it planned to send up to a metric ton there; Nevada sued, and learned through the suit that DOE had already shipped half of it while seemingly negotiating in good faith. In a 2020 settlement DOE agreed to remove that half ton from Nevada by the end of 2026 and to send no more (WB: Nevada Attorney General).

The site was also offered other countries' material. In 2014 DOE began assessing whether to take Germany's spent pebble-bed reactor fuel: graphite spheres containing American-supplied highly enriched uranium (WB: Federal Register). The site's own Citizens Advisory Board voted against it in 2017 (WB: CAB). By 2022 the German state concerned had chosen to store it at home instead (WB: SRS Watch).

Plutonium from a Japanese research assembly did come, through Charleston in 2016 (WB: SRS Watch). In 2021 NNSA (National Nuclear Security Administration) dropped its plan to send it on to New Mexico. It decided instead to process the plutonium at Savannah River and store it there until a geologic repository exists (WB: Federal Register).

The site's weapons work never fully ended. Work with radioactive tritium continued throughout (WB).

In 2019 the Defense Nuclear Facilities Safety Board warned that a fire, crane drop or explosion in the tritium facilities could cause acute radiation sickness or death in a significant number of people (WB: Federal Register). NNSA's administrator replied, "I do not accept Recommendation 2019-2" (WB: Federal Register).

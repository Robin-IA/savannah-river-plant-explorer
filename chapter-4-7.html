Ecology and the SRS

The land the plant took is now a large managed forest, and that is the good news. The Forest Service still runs it for DOE (Department of Energy).

In 2021 it thinned more than 3,000 acres, burned about 24,000 acres under control to keep the forest healthy, and sold timber from it. The red-cockaded woodpeckers kept coming back, from 3 active groups in 1985 to 150 in 2021, and golden eagles were seen on the site (WB: Forest Service, 2021).

The ecology laboratory restored wetlands for the rare gopher frog in 2021 (WB: DOE), and in 2023 released the young of box turtles seized from illegal collectors (WB: DOE). The forest even helps with cleanup: a Forest Service project uses trees to draw off water contaminated with radioactive tritium, about 9.5 million gallons in 2021 (WB: Forest Service).

The bad news is that the plant's radioactivity is still in its animals. The site's deer hunts, open to the public every autumn since 1965, are still held, and every deer and hog killed is taken to a check station and tested for radioactive cesium-137 before the hunter can keep it.

The site's limit is a radiation dose of 22 millirem a year to the hunter (WB: SRS, October 2026). Earlier rules said no animal is released if its radioactive cesium would put the hunter over the site's yearly radiation limit (WB: srs.gov hunt rules).

The site and its creeks stay closed to the public, fishing included. In 2021 the Forest Service replaced the trespass warning signs along thirty miles of the site's boundary on the Savannah River (WB: Forest Service).

Some fish below the site still carry radioactivity. South Carolina still advises people not to eat bowfin from the river below Stevens Creek, and notes that some fish contain radioactive cesium-137 and strontium-90 (WB: South Carolina advisory).

Georgia judges its mercury limits protective enough that it sets none for radioactivity (WB: Georgia guide). The site estimated that someone eating fish from its creek mouths in 2024 would get at most about 0.24 millirem of radiation, all from cesium-137 (WB: SRS).

What comes next for the site is already being planned.

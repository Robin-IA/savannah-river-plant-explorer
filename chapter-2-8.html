SRS and Ecology

In 1972 the site became the first National Environmental Research Park (WB: srs.gov 1970s). The ecology laboratory's work on reactor cooling water began in 1969, once satellite imagery had made the water temperatures impossible to keep secret (WB: Ecological Society of America), and became the Thermal Ecology symposia (IA).

The cooling water did real damage. A 1987 environmental impact statement found that K reactor's hot water had altered 670 acres of forested wetland along Pen Branch. C reactor's had altered about 1,147 acres, some 60 percent, along Four Mile Creek, where open water and marsh had replaced hardwood forest (IA: DOE, 1987). Restarting L reactor meant sending hot water into the Steel Creek swamp again (DL: Senate hearing, 1983).

Wildlife was in trouble too. In 1984 the wood stork was listed as endangered, and the L-Reactor study recorded 102 of them feeding near the Steel Creek delta the summer before (IA: DOE, 1984). In 1985 the site's red-cockaded woodpeckers were down to four birds; at DOE's (Department of Energy's) request, Forest Service scientists began carving artificial nest cavities and moving birds in from other forests (DL: Forest Service).

When the reactors stopped for good in 1992, the plant that had made bomb materials for almost four decades became a cleanup site, as Chapter 3 tells.

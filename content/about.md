<h2>The project</h2>
<p>The Savannah River Plant was built by Du Pont for the Atomic Energy Commission from 1950 on land in Aiken and Barnwell counties, South Carolina, and made plutonium and tritium for nuclear weapons until its reactors were shut down in 1988. Renamed the Savannah River Site in 1989, it has since been a place of clean-up, waste treatment, research and, again, plutonium work.</p>
<p>The story is told in three acts: building the plant, 1950–1969; the reactors under scrutiny, 1970–1992; and the years after bomb making, 1992 to now. It was written for <a href="https://archive.org/details/democracys-library">Democracy's Library</a>, whose aim is to be the most complete source for government documents, and it shows how much of one government site's history sits inside the Internet Archive, and where.</p>

<h2>Dates covered</h2>
<p>From the Atomic Energy Commission's decision in 1950 to the present. The sources themselves run from 1950 newspapers to web pages captured in 2026.</p>

<h2>Topics covered</h2>
<p>The choice of site and the towns moved off it, among them Ellenton and Dunbarton; building the plant; the reactors and separations plants; early ecology and the Savannah River Ecology Laboratory; the L-Reactor restart and lawsuit; the 1988 disclosures and shutdowns; the change from Du Pont to Westinghouse; liquid waste and the glass-making plant; sealing the P and R reactors in place; the MOX plant and plutonium pits; the national laboratory; and how the site is remembered.</p>

<h2>When the numbers were taken</h2>
<p>Every source was checked live on 6 November 2026: each archive.org item was fetched, each Democracy's Library label was re-tested against the collection's scope, and each Wayback capture and outside link was opened. The archives keep growing, so a source labelled IA today may be in Democracy's Library later.</p>

<h2>What is left out</h2>
<p>Sources the story still needs, such as the rest of the <em>New York Times</em> series of autumn 1988 and the &ldquo;thirty incidents&rdquo; report itself, are tracked as <a href="https://github.com/Robin-IA/savannah-river-plant-explorer/issues?q=is%3Aissue+label%3Asource-gap">open issues on GitHub</a>. Facts that could not be traced to a source are left out of the story until one is found.</p>

<h2>How it was built</h2>
<p>Researched from archive.org search and full-text search, the Wayback Machine's CDX server and the Archive-It and OSTI catalogues. The story and the source lists are written in Markdown, and a short Python script turns them into these pages. The timeline is drawn from the same story, event by event.

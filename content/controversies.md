<h2 id="the-arguments">The Arguments</h2>
<p>The plant&rsquo;s history has been argued over from the start, and the story sets out each argument with its sources rather than settling it:</p>
<ul class="controversies">
  <li><strong>The land.</strong> Whole towns, Ellenton and Dunbarton among them, were moved off the site from 1951. Most of the farms were worked by tenants, most of the farm workforce was African American, and the count of people displaced is still disputed. <a href="index.html#the-towns-inside-the-fence">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Who got hired.</strong> A 1951 House inquiry found that jobs went through the building-trades unions, turning applicants away at the gate, and the National Urban League recorded Black workers kept out of the skilled trades. <a href="index.html#building-the-plant">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>What the plant released early on.</strong> The CDC&rsquo;s dose reconstruction found 1950s iodine releases about four times what the site reported; a 1955 contamination blamed on Nevada fallout was later argued to be a reactor accident; workers from 1953 to 1972 were eventually compensated without individual dose reconstruction. <a href="index.html#radioactive-releases">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Restarting L reactor.</strong> In the 1980s buildup DOE moved to restart L reactor on an environmental assessment; the Natural Resources Defense Council sued, and in 1983 a court ruled that a full impact statement was needed, though it did not stop the restart. <a href="index.html#l-reactor-comes-back">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Groundwater and the state.</strong> Seepage basins contaminated the groundwater, including employees&rsquo; drinking-water wells, and the state regulator said the plant was at first reluctant to accept its authority. <a href="index.html#under-the-ground">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Tritium and Georgia.</strong> Large tritium releases in 1974, 1984 and 1987, downstream drinking-water testing, and Georgia&rsquo;s objection to burying waste above its main aquifer. <a href="index.html#radioactivity-and-community-safety">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Secret reactor accidents.</strong> In 1988 an internal list of thirty serious reactor incidents became public. The official history reads it as a record of improving safety, the congressional investigator who obtained it as evidence of concealment, and the <em>New York Times</em> reported accidents kept secret for up to 31 years. <a href="index.html#19871988-the-reactors-stop">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>What was released.</strong> Tritium from K reactor reached the Savannah River in 1991, and the CDC&rsquo;s dose reconstruction later counted about 3,000 release incidents involving tritium. <a href="chapter-2.html#a-name-change">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Worker health research and safety.</strong> In 1989 senators challenged DOE&rsquo;s control of research on its own workers&rsquo; health; GAO found earthquake weaknesses; the Par Pond dam scare of 1991 exposed contaminated sediment. <a href="chapter-2.html#a-name-change">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Waste left in the ground.</strong> What tank waste may stay buried was settled by Section 3116 of the 2005 defense authorization act, which passed by one vote. <a href="index.html#the-tanks">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The salt waste plants.</strong> In-Tank Precipitation was abandoned after almost half a billion dollars, and its replacement ran years late and over budget; DOE later redefined high-level waste to send some to Texas. <a href="index.html#the-tanks">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Plutonium coming and going.</strong> Governor Hodges&rsquo;s 2002 fight to block plutonium from Rocky Flats; DOE&rsquo;s secret shipment of half a ton to Nevada; proposals to bring in German and Japanese material; DOE&rsquo;s rejection of the safety board&rsquo;s tritium warning. <a href="index.html#plutonium-mox-and-pits">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>MOX and pits.</strong> DOE&rsquo;s 2016 baseline put the mixed-oxide fuel plant at $17.17 billion; DOE ended it in 2018&ndash;19 while South Carolina sued to hold the government to its promises; DOE&rsquo;s 2020 decision to make plutonium pits at the site is being challenged in federal court. <a href="index.html#plutonium-mox-and-pits">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Sick workers.</strong> Worker studies kept finding leukemia, compensation claims moved slowly and records had gaps; payments have since passed $2 billion. <a href="index.html#sick-workers">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The neighbors and the river.</strong> Federal studies found no cancer excess they could tie to the site, while an advocacy group disagreed; people who fish the river are warned about mercury, and some fish carry radioactive cesium and strontium. <a href="index.html#the-neighbors-and-the-river">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Growth without a town.</strong> The plant brought more than 10,000 newcomers by 1951 but no government town; schools ran double shifts, and under segregation one town put off a school for Black children. Decades later the cuts took away more than half the jobs, and the neighbors still fight to keep the payments made in place of taxes. <a href="chapter-1.html#living-with-the-plant">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Cooling water and the wetlands.</strong> Hot water from the reactors destroyed hundreds of acres of floodplain forest along Pen Branch, Four Mile Creek and Steel Creek, while the site&rsquo;s red-cockaded woodpeckers fell to four birds; both have since been the subject of long restoration work. <a href="chapter-2.html#srs-and-ecology">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>The ecology laboratory.</strong> Funding cuts in 2006 and 2007 brought layoffs, a rebuke from the site&rsquo;s own Citizens Advisory Board and a House hearing. <a href="index.html#the-laboratories">Read it in the story</a>.</li>
</ul>

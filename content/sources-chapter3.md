## 3. Liquid waste, DWPF, salt, Saltstone, Section 3116

## Key facts (each with its source)

**Turn to cleanup**
- Between 1987 and 1989, Du Pont announced its withdrawal, the last reactors were shut down, and Westinghouse was selected. The site's emphasis then shifted to environmental management rather than production. Source: *SRS at Fifty* Ch. 20 (#1).

**Contractors**
- WSRC ran liquid waste "for nearly two decades" until SRR took over at 12:01 a.m. on July 1, 2009. Source: #13.
- SRNS took over management and operations from WSRC on July 31 / August 1, 2008. Source: #12.

**DWPF**
- Construction began in late 1983, and radioactive operations began in March 1996. Source: #20.
- O'Leary started radioactive operations on March 12, 1996. Source: #23.
- 1,177 canisters had been poured by about 2001. Source: #21.
- The 4,000th canister was poured on December 31, 2015, out of about 8,000 expected. Source: #22.
- By 2026, just over 4,500 canisters had been filled. Source: #23.
- **Discrepancy:** #23 calls 4,500 "more than half" of the canisters expected, which agrees with #22's 8,000 only loosely.

**Tank inventory and closure**
- About 36 million gallons, 49 tanks, about 421 million curies. Source: #20, about 2006.
- Tanks 17 and 20 were closed in 1997, the first in the nation. Tanks 18 and 19 followed in 2012. Source: #24.
- Tank 12, the eighth closure, was completed on April 27, 2016. Source: #22.
- Tank 14 reached "preliminary cease waste removal" in May 2026, the eighth old-style tank in two years. Source: #26.

**Section 3116**
- Graham says he authored it and that it "passed by a single vote". Source: #24.
- The salt-waste determination was published on January 24, 2006. Source: #35.

**Salt waste and Saltstone**
- Saltstone has operated since June 1990. Source: #28.
- The interim salt process (ISDP) has run since April 2008. Source: #27.
- SWPF set record throughput in FY2021. Source: #29.

**P and R reactor decommissioning**
- ISD of P and R ran from October 2009 to September 2011. Source: #44.
- ARRA compressed the closure schedule from FY2016 to FY2011. Source: #39.
- The closure event took place on about September 30, 2011. Source: #39.
- **Discrepancy:** SRS's 2008 release says P Reactor's final shutdown was in **1988** (#40). The OSTI paper says P was shut down in **1991** (#44). Resolve this against a third source.

**ARRA**
- SRR received $200 million in ARRA funds in September 2009 and spent it on 41 projects. Source: #43.

**MOX**
- DOE's 2016 baseline put the total cost at $17.17 billion, with completion in 2048. Source: #54.
- The 4th Circuit vacated the injunction on January 8, 2019, for lack of standing. Source: #56.
- MOX workers' job fair, December 6, 2018. Source: #59.
- At least 1 metric ton of plutonium had been removed from SC by August 7, 2019. Source: #57.

**Pits**
- ROD for pit production at SRS: November 5, 2020. Source: #66.
- A NEPA suit by SRS Watch and others is pending in D.S.C. as 1:21-cv-01942. Source: #68.

**SRNL**
- Called "the country's newest national lab" in a July 8, 2004 release. Source: #70.
- The exact date of the 2004 designation is **unsourced** here.

**SREL**
- The grant was cut 47% (from $7.748 million to $4.3 million) for FY2006, with layoffs on September 30, 2005. Source: #78.
- FY2007 funding fell to $1 million against an expected $4.5 million. Source: #77.
- House Science held a hearing on July 19, 2007. Source: #75.

**Heritage**
- The first historic markers inside the SRS fence were placed on March 17, 2008. Source: #40.
- The SRS Museum ribbon-cutting was on October 28, 2014, with public opening due in 2015. Source: #83.
- NNSA gave $300,000 for the tritium exhibit in 2020. Source: #85.
